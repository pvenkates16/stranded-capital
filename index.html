Stranded Capital – couplings across sectors, geographies, and drivers

How to use this graph

Navigation

  • Pinch or scroll to zoom in/out.
  • Drag the background to pan around.
  • Drag a node to reposition it — the layout will settle again around your change.

Tapping things

  • Tap a node — highlights its direct connections and opens a detail panel at the bottom with what the node is and how many connections it has.
  • Tap a relationship (line) — highlights its two endpoints and opens a detail panel explaining the mechanism: whether it's causal (one-way) or symbiotic (two-way), plus a plain-language description of how the coupling works. Lines have an invisible wider tap zone so they're easier to hit than they look.
  • Tap empty background — clears any highlight and closes the detail panel.

Node color coding

Sector (Energy, Transport, Industry, Buildings, Agriculture, Finance)
Region, within a sector
Capital type (Produced, Human, Natural, Financial, Institutional)
Candidate bridge node — named hypothesis (top 3 strongest couplings)
Candidate bridge node — unlabeled, implied but not yet nameable (pulses)
Latent driver — a named common cause (e.g. climate change, sustainability PR, tech revolution) feeding several sectors at once
Latent cluster ("L?") — an unnamed grouping found mathematically via modularity optimization on the coupling network, not assumed

Relationship color coding

Physical — sector → region risk pathway
Litigation — sector → region risk pathway
Market — region → capital type, weak/derived driver
Interconnect — sector ↔ sector coupling; line width scales with coupling strength
Latent driver → the sectors it feeds
Latent cluster → its member sectors

Solid vs. dashed/dotted lines

Solid = established, direct causal drivers within the hierarchy (Physical, Litigation). Dashed/dotted = weaker, derived, structural, or latent relationships (Market, sector interconnections, latent drivers, latent clusters) — the dash pattern itself is a signal that the relationship is less direct than a solid line.

Arrowheads

Single arrowhead = causal, one-way relationship. Double arrowhead (both ends) = symbiotic, two-way relationship — currently used only on Finance edges, since capital flows out as investment and risk/return flows back.

Other visual cues

  • Line thickness on interconnect edges is proportional to coupling strength — thicker means more tightly coupled.
  • Pulsing red "?" nodes flag a mathematically implied relationship that doesn't have a name yet — a prompt to investigate, not a finished finding.
  • Clustering position is dynamic: sectors with stronger coupling are pulled physically closer together by the simulation, so proximity on screen is itself a signal.
  • Dimmed elements after a tap are everything not connected to what you selected — a quick way to trace one node or relationship's footprint through the whole graph.
